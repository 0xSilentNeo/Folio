**Test build.** Download `Folio-1.23.1.ipa` below and sideload it. It updates 1.23.0. It's a pre-release until you've checked it on your phone.

Each change below says what it rests on: your 5 Nov report, a measurement, the source of the tools involved, or a document.

### Widgets
- **What your report shows:** 1.23.0 wrote the widgets' files to the Keychain group "QEFBZCM9LG.dev.silentneo.folio" and got error -34018 ("missing entitlement") every time; your signing profile lists no Keychain groups at all.
- **Why:** KSign signs with zsign, which copies the profile's entitlements unchanged onto every binary, the app's and the widget's alike (zsign's source, openssl.cpp and archo.cpp; another zsign-based tool's maintainer says the same). So the app and the widget get the same entitlements, and with them the same default Keychain group, but not a group with that name.
- **Now:** the files go in the default Keychain group, named by neither side, which Apple documents as the one a query without a group uses.
- **Not yet confirmed:** that your widget can read them. The log says: "[widget] … the widget last read them: …", with the widget's own group in that line. If it still says "never", the next step is an App Group from a profile that has one (your certificate's seller), or a signing tool that registers one with your own Apple ID.

### Control Center and Lock Screen artwork
- **What your report shows:** the picture was the episode still at 480×269 px, the size Folio asks for in lists.
- **Now:** Control Center asks IMDb (and TMDB) for the same picture at 1400 px; both serve any width from the same address.

### The OpenGL renderer and your 4K Dolby Vision file
- **What your report shows:** on OpenGL the decoder switched between VideoToolbox and software decoding and HDR on and off many times a second, for as long as you waited.
- **Found in the simulator** with a 4K 10-bit HDR10 clip: whenever the decoder restarts, mpv reports no colour information for a moment ("video gamma: (none)", now in the log). Folio took that for an SDR video and switched HDR off, which restarted the decoder again, round and round.
- **Now:** a missing value is ignored until the file reports its own, and if HDR frames fail for a file they aren't tried again until the next one. In the simulator the clip now starts with one switch and plays to the end.
- **Needs your phone:** whether that file now plays on OpenGL, and how smoothly.

### Anime4K on Metal
- **The cost, from the shader files and Anime4K's own docs:** the ×2 upscalers run when the video is shown at more than 1.2× its size. A 1080p video on your screen is shown at 1.22× (2347×1320), so mpv built a 3840×2160 image every frame, ran A+A's second restore pass on that 4K image ("shaders applied after a x2 upscaling step will take four times the processing time"), then shrank it straight back to 2347 px. Anime4K's docs also say the doubled modes are meant only for ×2 scaling or more.
- **Now:** when the video is shown at less than 1.5× (1080p and up on this phone), only the restore stages run, at the video's own size; 720p and below keep the full chain. When the phone reaches "serious" heat, the restore stages only, at any size (Apple's thermal guidance: reduce GPU work). The log says which chain runs.
- **Leak?** In your report memory rose about 650 MB when Anime4K was turned on, rose about 460 MB more over the next 7 minutes, and was all freed when the player closed (1319 MB at close, 618 MB in the next file). So nothing outlives the player, though it does grow while Anime4K runs. The phone went from "nominal" to "serious" heat in that session. The lighter chain should change both; the next report will tell.
- **Control Center lag:** iOS draws its own UI on the same GPU. Apple offers no way to lower an app's GPU priority, so the remedy is less GPU work, as above.

### The time bar on iOS 27
- **What your report shows:** pressing a menu button made the glass capsule behind the three buttons swell and shrink, up to 13 sizes in one press ("capsule at 18…176 → 13…181 → 20…174"). The time bar is laid out next to the capsule, so it shrank and grew with it. Opening a menu also lifts its button out of the bar, which Folio took for "the buttons don't fit" and widened the bar by 12 pt.
- **Now:** the bar follows the capsule only once it has settled (0.35 s without change) and never while a menu is up.

### Pinch to zoom
- The picture follows your fingers between Fit and Fill, with a little give at the ends, then springs to the nearer one when you let go, and mpv takes over the framing. It used to change only when your fingers lifted.
- Needs your phone: how it feels (the simulator records the video picture as black).

### Not changed yet, and why
- **PiP and Notification Centre on Metal.**
  - **Notification Centre:** on iOS 27, fully opening it sends the app to the background about 0.8 s in; Control Center doesn't (Apple Developer Forums thread 845964, reproduced on the iOS 27 simulator). Your report shows exactly that: background 0.7 s after resign-active, then "video output off", then a 484 ms hold when the output was rebuilt. Metal may not draw in the background (Apple's "Preparing your Metal app to run in the background").
  - **The reference player's IPA** uses mpv's OpenGL ES renderer into pixel buffers enqueued into one AVSampleBufferDisplayLayer, which is both the on-screen video and the PiP source. Starting PiP only changes the render size, and it doesn't react to Notification Centre at all. Folio's OpenGL renderer is built the same way, which is why it was made in 1.22.0; its HDR loop is fixed above.
  - **Removing the pause on Metal** means drawing Metal frames into such a layer too, a large engine change, proposed separately rather than patched here.
- **Tab switch zoom:** measured in the simulator, no frame over 25 ms in any switch and no jump at the start. Tab switches now log their frame times, so the next report shows what happens on your phone.
- **Button hitches:** in the simulator a pause takes about 1 ms on the main thread and the frames stay under 34 ms, so there's nothing to fix there yet. The toolbar's capsule resizing (above) was a measured cause on your phone. Every player button now logs its frame times, so the next report shows the rest.
