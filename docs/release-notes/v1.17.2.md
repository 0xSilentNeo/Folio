### Switching titles fast
- **A page you're leaving no longer takes your taps, and taps meant for Home reach Home.** A title page that zooms back into its tile stays on screen over Home for the whole zoom. SwiftUI's zoom transition is interruptible, so the page kept receiving touches. Your 27 Oct log shows this at 19:12:55: the page started going back, and 114 ms later a tap played one of its episode rows. 1.17.1 tried to ignore those taps at the SwiftUI level, which only takes effect at SwiftUI's next update, so this one got through.
  - Now the page's view stops taking touches the moment it starts going back. This uses UIKit's `isUserInteractionEnabled`, which applies to the very next touch. Per Apple's docs, hit testing then skips the page, so the tap goes to Home underneath and opens what you tapped.
  - **Tested:** FASTSWITCH_RESULT
- **Taps on a page that is still zooming in work again.** 1.17.1 ignored them, which is why your buttons "were not getting registered". A swipe across a tile or an episode row still doesn't count as a tap.
- **How Infuse does it** (notes in your Infuse repo): it opens title pages with UIKit's standard push, not a zoom, keeps the system bar visible, and throttles taps to one per 300 ms. Folio keeps the zoom and stops the leaving page from taking touches instead.

### Leaving the app (Notification Centre, Control Centre, Home Screen)
- **The buffer survives a pull-down of Notification Centre.** Pulling it down sends the app to the background for a moment. While playing, Folio used to switch the video track off at once, and switching it back on reads the whole buffer again from the network.
  - Now only the video output goes, the same swap PiP does, which keeps the track and its buffer. Coming back builds the output again from the buffer.
  - If you're still away after 15 s with sound playing on, the video track goes off as before, so background listening doesn't decode video.
  - **Simulator test** (away for 3 s while playing, then back): AWAY_RESULT
- **Fixed: software decoding after returning to a paused video** (a bug from 1.17.1). The decoder rebuild ran at the very moment the app became active. Your log shows it falling back to software decoding for the rest of the file, using far more CPU. It's gone: coming back now builds a new video output and a new hardware decoder, as above.
